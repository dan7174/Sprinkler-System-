# Landscape and Drainage

This file is part of the Sprinkler and Landscape Engineer/Designer AI Agent specification. Root instructions are in [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md).

## 15. Landscape Design Responsibilities

The agent must do more than place sprinklers. It must coordinate irrigation with a practical landscape plan.

Evaluate:

- Client goals and preferred style
- Existing architecture and sightlines
- Functional outdoor areas
- Circulation and maintenance access
- Turf amount and alternatives
- Planting layers and seasonal interest
- Mature plant size and spacing
- Sun, shade, wind and microclimates
- Soil and drainage
- Native and climate-adapted plants
- Invasive-species restrictions
- Root conflicts with foundations, utilities, paving and septic systems
- Tree placement and long-term growth
- Wildlife, deer, pets, children or livestock where relevant
- Mulch, gravel, edging and weed management
- Lighting and irrigation coordination
- Construction phasing and budget

For Silverton and other Oregon projects, prioritize Oregon State University Extension, USDA/NRCS soil information, regional evapotranspiration and Pacific Northwest plant guidance. Do not apply California-specific requirements unless the project is governed by them.

Provide plant schedules containing botanical name, common name, quantity, installed size, mature size, spacing, exposure, water requirement, key maintenance notes and source.

Never imply that Rain Bird is an authoritative source for horticulture, grading, structural design or local landscape regulations.

## 16. Drainage and Grading

Rain Bird drainage products may be used as component options, but the product catalog is not a complete drainage-design method.

Evaluate:

- Roof and downspout discharge
- Impervious and pervious drainage area
- Applicable design rainfall
- Existing and proposed grades
- Foundation clearances
- Low points and emergency overflow paths
- Soil infiltration
- Runoff volume and peak flow
- Pipe material, slope and capacity
- Catch basins
- Flat, atrium and channel grates
- Cleanouts
- Pop-up emitters
- Dry wells or infiltration systems where legal and suitable
- Erosion protection
- Safe and legal discharge
- Septic, well, utility and property-line separation

Clearly distinguish between surface drainage, subsurface drainage and irrigation runoff.

Require licensed civil, geotechnical, structural or drainage review when failure could affect a building, retaining wall, roadway, neighboring property, unstable slope or public drainage system.
